# A merchant account from the first stockroom to exit

A small retailer needs one account for the business, with a location for each place that holds stock. Give each person their own sign-in and a membership describing what they may do for that merchant and at which locations. A shop owner who also works at a relative’s business can use one identity with two independent memberships. Keep the business name and current location visible wherever a worker can change stock.

## What existing products demonstrate

- Shopify offers a switcher for stores associated with one login. Square lets a manager invite a worker, assign permissions and locations, and attribute shared-register actions to individual workers. These are precedents for a switcher and scoped invitations. [Shopify store switching](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/logging-in/store-switcher) · [Square team invitations and locations](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8356-add-and-manage-team-members)
- Shopify provides a distinct ownership-transfer workflow, including a transfer to an existing user. Its trial documentation describes carrying work forward on a paid plan. Its closure guidance calls attention to outstanding orders and records the owner may need to export. [Shopify ownership changes](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/manage-orgs-and-stores/change-transfer-ownership) · [Trial conversion](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/pricing-plans/free-trial) · [Outstanding obligations and export before closure](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/manage-billing/billing-considerations-pause-deactivate-store)
- Slack’s enterprise migration asks the existing workspace owner to approve the move and documents the impact on identity provisioning and access to audit logs. An enterprise WMS move also needs a named decision-maker and a record of what will travel with the merchant. [Slack enterprise migration](https://slack.com/help/articles/115002532808-Migrate-workspaces-to-an-Enterprise-organization)

## A shift with two memberships

The following screens are a proposed Spanish-language flow for a fictional retailer.

| Moment | Screen and action | Result to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elena starts the business account | **Crear negocio** → “Nombre del negocio: Textiles Sol” → **Agregar ubicación** → “Depósito Centro”. The confirmation names Elena as the owner. | One merchant and one location exist. She can prepare a catalog and count stock. A retry after the confirmation screen times out returns the same account. Keep sample data out of the live ledger. |
| Elena invites Ana | **Invitar al equipo** → “Ana · Operadora · Depósito Centro”. Ana receives “Textiles Sol te invita a recibir y preparar pedidos en Depósito Centro” and accepts under her own login. | A pending invite confers no access. Accepting binds the role and location to Ana’s membership. The owner can cancel an unaccepted invite. Ana can receive and pick there. Billing, owner transfer and other locations stay outside her role. |
| Ana also helps at a second business | **Elegir negocio** lists “Textiles Sol · Operadora · Depósito Centro” and “Finca Norte · Encargada · Galpón”. Both use SKU 0042 and have a receipt 218. | Choosing a merchant opens its own tasks, permitted locations and search results. A stale link names the intended merchant and requires an authorized switch before showing its receipt. The receiving screen displays the merchant and site beside the order number. |
| Ana taps Finca Norte while confirming a Sol receipt | “Quedan 2 lecturas de Textiles Sol en este equipo. ¿Qué quieres hacer?” Offer **Seguir en Textiles Sol** and, after a durable local save, **Guardar y cambiar**. If a save fails, preserve the open task and offer recovery. | Pending readings retain Sol’s merchant, site, original actor and operation IDs. Reconnection routes them to Sol for authorization and reconciliation, even if Ana is viewing Finca. Returning to Sol resumes the task. A removed membership sends its unresolved observations for supervisor review through the agreed recovery process. |
| Ana signs out of the shared device | “Textiles Sol · 1 recepción pendiente de confirmar. Entregar turno”. The incoming worker signs in and, if authorized, accepts the task. | The history preserves Ana’s two observations and the incoming worker’s later decisions. A different merchant’s worker cannot inspect them. ## Who can act when the account changes

| Event | Proposed policy to test with the owner |
| --- | --- |
| Worker leaves | Deactivate only the affected membership, revoke its sessions and device grants, and remove it from merchant selection. Reassign open picks, holds and cases to a named supervisor. Keep historical authorship and check disconnected devices for pending observations under that identity. The worker’s other merchant memberships continue independently. |
| Business changes owner | The current owner reauthenticates, names an eligible successor and sees the exact business being transferred. The successor explicitly accepts. Complete the owner change atomically and choose the previous owner’s continuing access. Until acceptance or cancellation, the existing owner remains accountable. Separately confirm billing contact, recovery contact, devices and connector credentials. Establish an evidence-reviewed recovery route if the sole owner is unreachable. |
| Trial converts to paid or expires | Convert the existing merchant in place with the same location IDs, ledger and invite history. Before trial expiry, show the owner pending pickups, returns, transfers and device queues. Let them review the transition and export. If the trial contains only demo stock, offer an explicit start with empty production balances. |
| Commercial suspension with goods in custody | Proposed restricted mode pauses new demand, customer promises and ordinary allocation. Authorized staff can see existing obligations, complete a verified pending pickup, record a return into hold, reconcile a prior handoff and export records. Tell the channels to stop offering new promises and expose any publisher lag. Label the screen “Cuenta restringida · 2 retiros y 1 devolución pendientes”. Each allowed physical event posts once. A suspension caused by compromised credentials needs a separately verified recovery operator and access path. |
| Merchant exits | Show a closing checklist by location covering staged orders, returns, in-transit stock, disputed guides and unsynchronized devices. Agree a cutoff, stop integrations from writing and retain their last acknowledged checkpoints. Supply an authorized export of catalog and units, per-site balances, movement history, open obligations, references, timestamps and ownership history in documented formats. Validate file counts and closing balances with the owner before ending operational access. Define export access, retention, deletion and recovery windows in the service terms. |
| Merchant moves to an enterprise installation | Keep the same business identity and movement references. Preview which sites, memberships, service accounts, authentication policies and records are moving. Require approval from the owner and destination administrator. At the agreed cutoff, drain or account for queued work, reconcile totals and open pickups, verify destination permissions and enable one writer. Rehearse a rollback that preserves any new movements. [Moving a merchant between storage layouts](../architecture/tenant-isolation.md#moving-a-merchant-between-layouts) · [Enterprise recovery and tenant moves](../architecture/enterprise-installation.md#upgrades-recovery-and-tenant-moves) |

**Pilot decision.** Ask the merchant who may invite, own, release stock during suspension and receive the closing export. Rehearse two businesses with identical receipt numbers, a lost connection, a revoked worker and one unclaimed pickup. Pass when both ledgers reconcile, the other merchant’s data stays inaccessible, and the incoming worker can name the owner of every unfinished physical task. Membership roles, suspension permissions and exit windows remain proposed product policies pending merchant and contractual review.
