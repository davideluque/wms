# Buy, adapt or build a warehouse workflow

For a small Venezuelan retailer, begin with the system that already receives its orders and records its stock. A scanner workflow, a pick list or a pickup status alone offers little evidence for building a new product. Odoo, ERPNext and Shopify each document working examples. The decision turns on whether staff can reconcile physical units, a customer handover and the existing accounting or carrier reference on the retailer’s own equipment.

## What the alternatives already do

Assign a named owner to update each record, fix one recurring process failure and time the workflow again. |

The product documentation establishes a workflow to test. Local account eligibility, the retailer’s contracted plans, fiscal compatibility, hardware support, carrier entitlements and implementation costs remain open until the merchant or supplier verifies them. For each credible option, obtain a dated quote covering the same sites, staff, equipment, training, support and data migration. Include the internal hours needed to operate any remaining manual steps.

## A reproducible pilot

1. **Choose one site and its incumbent.** Ask the owner, receiving clerk and dispatcher for ten recent orders, a stock export, several labels and redacted carrier and accounting records. Include a stock discrepancy, a partial order and a store pickup. Use the retailer’s share of these exceptions to avoid overvaluing a rare case.
2. **Run the same order twice.** First observe the current process, then test the closest existing product or configuration in a test company. Receive two cases of 12, hold two damaged units and prepare five for pickup. The expected balance before handover is 17 available, two held and five staged. After collection, five have left custody. Separately dispatch a parcel, enter one carrier guide, lose the acknowledgement and recover the original reference. Have the next shift trace both orders.
3. **Measure outcomes.** Compare active minutes and manual entries per order, assistance needed, stock differences by location, duplicate postings, wrong handovers, missing guides and time to close the daily reconciliation. For an outage-sensitive site, record the live site's interruption frequency and compare recovery time using a planned simulation. Record the setup time and who could maintain each option.
4. **Test willingness to adopt.** Ask the decision-maker to name the recurring loss worth paying to remove, expected benefit, training window, pilot users and spending limit. Agree on the smallest meaningful improvement before revealing trial results. Run the winning workflow alongside the existing process for one receiving and dispatch cycle, using a shadow record or test tenant so stock and fiscal documents post only once.

## Decision after the trial

- **Buy or configure** when an existing system meets the merchant’s acceptance criteria, reconciles stock and fits its rollout budget. Document the settings, train the next shift and close the product experiment for that site.
- **Adapt** when one bounded gap causes most of the observed cost. For example, if operators retype every parcel into a supported carrier template, trial exporting the validated rows and importing guide numbers while the merchant’s ERP continues to own stock. Measure time saved and support effort before extending the integration.
- **Build a focused pilot** when the best configured alternative still fails a frequent, costly task and another independent retailer can demonstrate the same failure. A candidate might combine Spanish floor capture, case-to-unit validation and recovery of an uncertain local dispatch. Reproduce the gap with real labels, a carrier sample and a shift handover. Seek a named operational sponsor and a commitment to run the trial before building a broader WMS.
- **Keep the current tools** when the revised process meets the thresholds, order volume cannot justify switching, or the owner cannot identify a recurring loss. A labeled staging shelf, a required guide reference or a daily reconciliation owner may be the complete outcome.
