# Traceability for packaged ice and farm deliveries

Pilot with a finished, identifiable lot whose units stay identifiable through receipt and dispatch. A distributor receiving sealed bags of edible ice can count bags against the supplier lot. A farm delivering labeled crates can provide its harvest or packing lot and the measured net weight where the buyer trades by weight. Clarify whether ice is a food or a cooling material, and whether the operator grows, packs, repacks or only distributes. If two farm lots are pooled or repacked, traceability requires the actual input-to-output links. Choose intact lots for the first pilot if the operator cannot supply that history.

## Guidance and local applicability

FAO/WHO Codex describes tracing food to its immediate source and next recipient as appropriate to the objective. Its general hygiene code discusses lot identification, recalls, temperature monitoring and checking and calibrating measuring devices. Codex describes its texts as voluntary international guidance. [Codex traceability principles](https://www.fao.org/input/download/standards/10603/CXG_060e.pdf) · [Codex General Principles of Food Hygiene](https://www.fao.org/fao-who-codexalimentarius/sh-proxy/pl/?lnk=1&url=https%253A%252F%252Fworkspace.fao.org%252Fsites%252Fcodex%252FStandards%252FCXC%2B1-1969%252FCXC_001e.pdf) · [Status of Codex texts](https://codex.fao.org/codex-texts/about-codex-texts)

Venezuela’s 2008 *Ley Orgánica de Seguridad y Soberanía Agroalimentaria* (decree 6.071) addresses traceability in article 67 for activities involving production and transformation of food. Article 75 addresses conservation conditions for each type of food, including during storage, transport and distribution. Article 81 refers to storage techniques in applicable rules. Ask the operator’s qualified local adviser which current provisions apply to its role, product and site, including retention, notification and transport obligations. Request the dated operating procedure, named decision-maker and sample documents. [Gaceta Oficial 5.889 Extraordinario, arts. 67 and 75, printed p. 42, FAOLEX scan](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ven83198.pdf#page=15) · [Article 81, printed p. 43](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ven83198.pdf#page=16)

## Minimum records and decisions

| Warehouse event | Record and operator action |
| --- | --- |
| Receipt | Record supplier and contact, original lot as printed, SKU, source delivery reference, receipt time, actual quantity, unit and location. Preserve the link if staff assign an internal lot ID. A grower’s “07” and a second supplier’s “07” are distinct lots. If a crate’s weight varies, store the counted crates and measured net weight separately. |
| Manual temperature check | Where the operator’s procedure calls for a check, capture measured value and unit, time observed, location, what was measured (e.g. storage-area air or shipment), linked lot or shipment, operator, device ID and method. Link the device’s calibration or verification record, date, evidence reference and next due date if specified. Preserve the original entry and log corrections. A late entry records both observation and entry time. A missing measurement remains explicitly unknown. |
| Hold and release | **Retener lote** records scope, quantity, bin, reason, author and time. Block affected units from allocation or dispatch, identify already staged parcels and tell staff where to physically segregate them. Show units already shipped on a separate follow-up list. Only the named quality decision-maker can record release, continued hold or disposition, with quantity, reason and evidence. A new reading alone leaves the hold in place. |
| Shipment and return | Capture actual lot quantities delivered into carrier or customer custody, recipient site, date, guide or delivery reference, movement ID and any partial balance. Keep ordered, shipped and invoiced quantities separately visible. A return references the original shipment line and enters held stock. Finance owns any credit, invoice or valuation update and links it to the physical transaction. |
| Alert and recall | A supplier notice, missing required reading, device with overdue evidence, or reading outside the operator’s approved rule opens a case for the assigned lead. Show the affected lot, location, evidence and next action. Group repeat alerts under the same unresolved case. Export source receipts, on-site and staged stock, transfers in transit, dispatched quantities by recipient, returns, corrections and unresolved contacts. An authorized person owns external notifications and keeps proof of acknowledgment. |

Ask the operator which person may set product-specific limits, monitoring intervals and disposition rules. Put their approved procedure and version beside the relevant checks. A handheld entry supports a point-in-time observation. Continuous coverage requires different evidence. Where loose retail sales cannot identify the final buyer, preserve the selling location, time window, lot linkage if available and quantity. Show the remaining contact gap to the recall lead.

## Tabletop recall of 120 bags

All quantities below are fictional. Supplier H delivered **120 sealed bags** of SKU H5 under lot H-17 and receipt R-40. The test begins after two wholesale shipments, before any external notifications. A second lot of H5 has its own balance and must remain identifiable.

| State before supplier notice | Bags | Evidence to produce |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Available at the depot | 45 | Rack count and lot label |
| Staged for customer C, still at depot | 10 | Pick ticket and staging bin |
| Held at receipt | 5 | Hold record and physical count |
| Handed to customer A | 35 | A’s receipt for dispatch D-71 |
| Handed to customer B | 25 | B’s receipt for dispatch D-72 |
| **Total received** | **120** | **45 + 10 + 5 + 35 + 25** |

1. Enter a mock supplier notice against H-17. Block the 45 available and 10 staged bags, preserve the five already held, and stop C’s dispatch. The depot now holds **60**. Gross shipped remains **60**, split between A and B. Report C as an affected open order with **zero** shipped. Verify the hold scope against the second lot.
2. A acknowledges the notice and returns 15 against D-71. Receive those 15 into hold. Under the test facts, A retains 20 and B retains 25. B’s acknowledgment is still pending. **75 held + 20 with A + 25 with B = 120**. The shipment history still shows 35 + 25 gross shipped, 15 returned and **45 net outside the depot**.
3. Produce the recall evidence pack and compare it with a physical count of 75 held. Distinguish **120 quantity-accounted-for**, **75 under depot control** and **one recipient acknowledgment pending**. If the counter finds 74, show a one-bag discrepancy, assign a recount and leave the case open. Never infer customer contact or goods recovery from an arithmetic balance.

During the exercise, time how long staff take to identify the source, block staged stock and assemble the recipient list. Check that a repeated return confirmation leaves the held count at 75 and that a second operator can trace each number from a document. Record actual results at the site before offering recall-readiness claims.
