# Counting stock while the shop is trading

A cycle count verifies a limited set of physical stock during ordinary operations. A practical starting policy for a small merchant is one short count assignment per shift, plus a spot count after a short pick, unexpected zero or disputed transfer. Rotate through lower-activity shelves as well as fast sellers. Adjust the workload after measuring time spent, overdue bins and repeat discrepancies. Microsoft Dynamics supports scheduled and spot counts, and allows reservations and outbound work while a location count is open. [Dynamics 365 cycle counting](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/warehousing/cycle-counting)

## Decide what the count covers

| Count assignment | Floor procedure | What the result establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One bin | Scan the location. Count all physical items, including unexpected variants, loose units and items on hold. Record separate lines by variant, unit and condition. Confirm an empty bin explicitly. | Stock observed in that bin. It can reveal a shirt placed in the wrong slot. Other bins remain unverified. |
| One item across a site | For a repeated shortage, visit each sales shelf, back room, returns area and pickup shelf that might hold the variant. Record which bins were checked and any goods moved during the search. | The site total only after completing the full scope. Five missing from the shelf and five found in staging may call for a bin transfer even when the site total agrees. |

Give one person responsibility for each physical area during the count. Hide the expected quantity and the previous result until they submit. Keep an explicit distinction between “counted zero,” “not visited” and “still counting.” A second device taking over should reopen the saved assignment and observed lines. A retry of a saved scan has the same operation ID. A deliberate scan of another identical unit has a new operation ID. For two people counting in parallel, assign disjoint bins or shelf sections. Oracle documents both blind counts and bin-level coverage for item counts. ## Reconcile sales and picks at the observation time

Record the quantity seen, count time, bin, counter and ledger position. Keep the scan time, physical handover time and upload time distinct. Compare the observation to the physical ledger at that moment, including movements known to have occurred before the observation. Apply verified subsequent movements once. A reservation alone changes availability. A physical pick into staging changes the source bin. A carry-out removes stock from the site. If the POS supplies only the site, investigate before assigning its sale to a particular bin. NetSuite likewise documents recording movements that occur while a count is in progress. [NetSuite inventory count snapshots](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2299731.html)

**A late till receipt can create a false two-unit loss.** The ledger shows 12 shirts. At 10:02 a cashier hands over two from the only shelf; the offline till saves the sale. At 10:04 a worker counts ten. At 10:06 another worker hands over one and records it immediately. At 10:10 the till uploads the two-unit sale. Comparing ten to the original twelve and immediately posting a loss of two would leave the final ledger at seven after both actual sales. Reconstructing events gives ten at count time and nine now. The correct count adjustment is zero. If the sequence or the till’s completeness cannot be established, keep the observation pending and recount that shelf briefly under controlled access. [Shopify POS offline sync](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/sell-in-person/shopify-pos/selling-offline/offline-features)

## Who can pick or approve while a count is pending?

- **Routine count with known movements.** Continue normal picks and sales. Briefly coordinate a handover with the counter when both workers reach for the same shelf. Record whether the goods were included in the observation. Publish confirmed movements normally.
- **Uncertain shortage or overlapping observations.** Pause new promises against the disputed item/bin and direct pickers to verified stock. Check existing reservations before rerouting them. A picker who physically finds fewer units records the actual pick and opens a shortage. Show the scope of the pause and a person responsible for clearing it. Leave unrelated items available.
- **Approval.** A sole owner may make a fresh blind recount, attach source evidence and sign a linked correction. A merchant with two staff can assign an independent counter or approver for material, repetitive or unexplained differences. The operator records who counted, who reviewed and when both roles belonged to one person. Let the merchant set escalation thresholds from stock value and past variance. ## Attribute the difference before posting it

Check for an unsynced sale, a pick staged elsewhere, an unreceived transfer, a case-to-each mismatch and goods held after a return. Record the supporting sale, transfer or count when the cause is known. A missing source transaction should post through its own workflow. When a recount confirms a real unexplained shortage, an authorized operator can post a count adjustment with the cause “unknown.” Preserve the observation, adjustment, reviewer and any later linked correction. If movements have arrived since review, recompute before posting and protect the adjustment against a duplicate submission.

Show signed unit variance by SKU and unit. For approval, also show absolute units and an estimated value using the merchant’s dated unit cost and currency, when available. A missing cost should stay visible as unknown. One expensive item can warrant review even with a small unit variance. Equal shortages and overages in different variants can cancel in a net total while both shelf balances are wrong. Report affected lines, gross variance value and repeat discrepancies alongside net value. Oracle likewise separates quantity and value tolerances. [Oracle cycle count approval criteria](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/supply-chain-and-manufacturing/25c/famml/how-cycle-count-approval-tolerances-are-calculated.html)

**Shift acceptance.** Replay the offline till example, send the same scanner submission twice, then misplace one size M shirt in the size L slot. A second-shift worker should be able to recover the pending count, explain nine shirts after both sales, identify the bin move, and post each justified correction once. Record minutes of interrupted picking, unresolved counts and how long disputed items remained unavailable.
