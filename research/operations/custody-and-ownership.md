# Whose goods can this order use?

For the first release, choose a merchant that operates its own warehouse and stores and owns the countable goods it offers for sale. Keep the merchant account, location, physical handoff and quantity on every relevant movement. Verify who owns the pilot goods during onboarding. If a shelf also holds supplier- or client-owned merchandise, identify it on the floor and exclude it from opening balances and customer promises. An account owner is a person authorized to administer the business account. That permission establishes no title to the items on a shelf. ## Three different arrangements

| Arrangement | What can be shared? | What must remain distinct? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One retailer, one warehouse and two stores | One merchant account, one inventory owner, a common catalog and authorized transfers. | Stock and commitments per site, plus custody while moving. Ten units at Store A do not make ten units ready for pickup at Store B. |
| Three independent retailer accounts | The software service and, in some cases, a person with three memberships. | Each merchant’s records, permissions, commercial orders and quantities. Identical SKUs and a shared street address do not authorize combining stock. |
| One third-party logistics operator (3PL), three client owners, one warehouse | A facility, dock, forklift and the operator assigned to perform work for specific clients. | Which client owns each unit, who may release it, whose order can reserve it and which client can see the report. One possible account design gives the 3PL a scoped operator view and each client access to its own records. |

GS1’s EPCIS guidance distinguishes the owning party, the party in physical possession and the location. Oracle WMS separately describes a retailer operating multiple facilities and a logistics provider handling client-company inventory in the same facility. These concepts help identify the parties on a movement. The contract determines when title passes. [GS1 EPCIS and CBV implementation guideline, section 5.2](https://ref.gs1.org/guidelines/epcis-cbv/2.0.0/) · [Oracle WMS companies and facilities](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/warehouse-management/26a/owsec/companies-and-facilities.html)

## Three movements, with the quantities kept separate

**Retailer transfer on its own truck.** Start with 12 sealed bags in the warehouse, zero on the truck and three at the shop. Load five against a transfer. Custody is now seven at the warehouse, five with the driver and three at the shop. The retailer still owns all **15**. The shop receives four, leaving **7 warehouse + 1 on truck + 7 shop = 15**. The final bag stays assigned to the driver or in-transit investigation until a count or handoff resolves it. The shop can offer only its seven received bags, assuming no holds or reservations. A dedicated truck stock location becomes useful if the driver also sells from it or holds stock between trips. For a single trip serving one store, ordinary transfer-in-transit and a driver reference can explain the same outstanding bag. [Retail transfer and short receipt](store-replenishment.md)

**Consignment sale.** A shop counts 12 identical units, four of its own and eight held for supplier V. Assume the agreement lets the shop sell as V’s agent and title passes directly from V to the customer at handover. Reserve three against V’s pool. All 12 remain at the shop, including five unreserved V units. On handover, the shop holds **4 own + 5 V = 9** and three have passed to the customer. Record the quantity sold for V and the settlement reference. Finance applies the agreement to calculate any payment. If the agreement instead requires the shop to acquire title before sale, record a distinct owner change for three units. The ownership split goes from **4 own + 8 V** to **7 own + 5 V**, still 12 at the site. Then deliver three from the shop-owned pool. The finishing shelf count is identical, but the ownership history and payable evidence differ. Odoo documents owner-specific stock and valuation. Dynamics illustrates a distinct ownership-change journal for vendor consignment. [Odoo consignment](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/warehouses_storage/advanced_operations_warehouse/owned_stock.html) · [Dynamics consignment and ownership changes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/inventory/consignment)

**3PL release.** A 3PL holds **6 client A + 4 client B + 2 client C = 12** units in one facility. A authorizes dispatch of five from A to A’s remote depot. Under this example’s contract, A retains title throughout. The 3PL hands five to a carrier against A’s release and a manifest. Its facility now holds **1 A + 4 B + 2 C = 7**. The five in carrier custody still belong to A. On confirmed receipt, A’s remote depot holds five. A second A order for two can reserve at most one at the 3PL. Using one of B’s four would violate the release instruction even if it has the same barcode. A missing label leaves the release pending until the operator can establish whose unit it is. Oracle’s model illustrates eligibility by client and facility. [Oracle WMS company hierarchy](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/warehouse-management/26d/owmol/parent-child-company-hierarchy.html)

## An item left at the counter without a matching sale

A customer brings a blue M shirt to Store B. Staff can scan its variant, but the receipt is missing and they have yet to establish which sale or handover it belongs to. Ask the merchant whether staff may take custody during that search. If the customer leaves with the shirt, the interaction adds zero units to the store. If the merchant accepts it for review, give the physical item a case reference, record its observed condition and receiving site, and tell the customer how to refer to the case. In the proposed pilot, a review case authorizes neither a payout nor a replacement promise.

| What staff verify | Change to Store B's record |
| --- | --- |
| B starts with four available blue M shirts. It accepts one more to investigate. | Staff can account for five shirts on site. Four remain available and one is in separately identified custody pending a decision. Assign the case to the returns lead. |
| The lead finds two fulfilled lines for the same variant, from different shipments. | Keep the item attached to the case while the lead checks the original purchase and remaining returnable quantities. Scanning the garment again or opening a candidate sale adds zero units. |
| Evidence establishes the sale, the merchant accepts the return and the shirt passes inspection. | Link the original case to the authorized return line, record the change of disposition and move that same shirt from hold to the saleable bin. Five shirts are now available. A replay of the channel return still leaves five. |
| Alternatively, the merchant declines the return and hands the shirt back. | Record handback against the case and close custody. Four merchant units remain available. If the shirt still sits behind the counter, keep the case open with a person responsible for it. |

Eligibility, merchant policy and evidence of a handover each answer a different question. Shopify's order-linked exchange flow queries eligible fulfillment lines and uses a particular fulfillment-line ID and quantity to initiate a return. A variant ID alone cannot select between two shipments. Shopify POS Pro also documents an unverified return workflow with its own staff, currency and gift-card requirements, a customer profile, and a restock choice at the POS location. If the merchant uses it, carry its external reference into the case, check the restock effect before publishing availability and retain the channel's prior financial settlement when an original sale is later identified. Agree how the merchant resolves a potential second claim. Keep necessary contact details in the authorized source. [Shopify exchange line selection](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/orders-fulfillment/returns-apps/manage-exchanges) · [Shopify POS unverified returns](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/sell-in-person/shopify-pos/order-management/unverified-returns) · [Physical and financial return evidence](returns-and-corrections.md)

## When to revisit the first-release assumption

- **For a truck**, ask for one load sheet, a short store receipt, a driver’s end-of-day count and any direct truck sale. Establish who held the outstanding piece, who may sell it and whether staff need a balance across multiple trips.
- **For consignment**, ask for a redacted agreement, supplier receipt, completed sale, settlement and return. Have the shop and supplier each identify the title-change event, eligible stock, loss responsibility, required evidence and who can authorize an adjustment. Reconcile physical quantity and amounts due independently.
- **For a 3PL**, ask two clients for release instructions for the same SKU, an operator’s pick and dispatch record, and the client-facing balances. Rehearse a short pick, wrong-owner label and returned parcel. Confirm how mixed stock is identified and whether commingling is expressly permitted. Test that each client sees only its information and that an operator’s permission for one client cannot release another’s unit. [Tenant isolation and request selection](../architecture/tenant-isolation.md#how-a-request-selects-its-merchant)

Extend the model only when a merchant supplies a recurring case that the pilot’s single-owner ledger cannot represent and can demonstrate the required authority and reconciliation. For an extension, owner identity must survive receipt, reservation, transfer, return, correction and export. Supplier identity alone cannot determine current ownership after a sale or title change. The balances above are tabletop cases for discovery.
