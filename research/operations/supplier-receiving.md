# Receiving a supplier delivery in a shop without an ERP

A shop owner can start an expected delivery from a supplier message or a notebook entry. Record the supplier, receiving site, expected date if known, item, quantity and unit. Keep the buyer’s reference or a copy of the message. An expected delivery gives the receiver something to compare with the truck. Stock changes only when staff record custody or a later disposition. Purchasing owns the decision to accept changed terms. The billing or administrative system owns invoices, tax and supplier credits. Shopify also links its commercial purchase order to a separate receiving transaction. [Shopify purchase orders and receiving](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/inventory-and-locations/purchase-orders/creating-inventory-transfers)

## The smallest useful receiving screen

1. **Find the delivery or start an unexpected arrival.** Show open expectations for the supplier and site. Give each physical arrival its own receipt ID, even when two trucks share an order or invoice. Record the supplier’s delivery-note number as written and any previous receipt with the same supplier, document type and number. Let the operator resume a receipt or confirm a distinct load using the date, packages and prior handover. Re-importing an identifiable source event or resending a saved line uses its original command ID.
2. **Count what arrived.** Record each actual item, observed pack, count, base-unit conversion, condition and receiving bin. Split lines by lot or expiry when the item requires them. Staff can enter a zero for an expected line after checking it. Leave untouched lines visibly unchecked. If the pack factor, variant or required date is unknown, record the physical packages in a marked hold area until someone verifies them.
3. **Separate usable goods from exceptions.** Put confirmed saleable units into the mapped receiving or storage bin and record holds in a distinct bin or stock state. A return awaiting the driver remains physically present and unavailable. Goods refused at the truck and kept by the driver have an exception and custody evidence, with zero stock posted to the shop. ERPNext likewise distinguishes accepted and rejected receiving warehouses. [ERPNext purchase receipt](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/purchase-receipt)
4. **Hand over the decision.** Save the counted lines before a manager reviews the difference. Give each unresolved quantity a reason, responsible person and next action. The owner can accept an excess, approve a substitute, request the balance, or close the remainder with a reason. Record the decision against the original expectation. Share the receipt and return references with whoever reconciles the supplier bill. [Microsoft partial and excess receipts](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/purchasing-how-record-purchases)

| What the receiver sees | What the WMS should preserve |
| --- | --- |
| No order or expectation | Supplier, arrival, packages, provisional contents and custody location. Hold the goods pending identification or acceptance. If a matching order arrives later, link it to this receipt once. |
| More of an ordered item | Actual quantity and excess quantity. Release the agreed portion. Keep the excess traceable and unavailable until the person authorized to accept the purchase decides. |
| Less of an ordered item | Exact shortage in the ordered unit or a verified base unit. Leave that balance open for a further delivery or record who agreed to close it. |
| A different brand, pack or variant | Store the actual SKU and proposed link to the expected line. Have the buyer confirm any permitted substitution. Preserve both identities and units. A new case size of the same underlying item can use its verified pack conversion. A different underlying SKU needs its own stock line. ## Five cases on the order, four bottles short at the dock

The owner expects **five cases of 12** of regular cooking oil, or **60 bottles**. The shelf starts empty. Under delivery note D-71, the receiver counts three sealed 12-bottle cases of lot A and two 10-bottle cases of lot B. Staff verify both pack sizes from their labels. Two lot-B bottles are damaged. The driver also leaves three bottles of a different variant that the owner has not approved.

| Step | Physical result | Order or supplier follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Receive R-41 | Lot A contributes 36 usable bottles. Lot B contributes 18 usable and 2 held. The other variant contributes 3 held. The shop holds **59 total**, of which **54** are available and **5** held. | Four of the original 60 bottles never arrived. Two more arrived damaged. The three bottles of the other SKU stay outside order fulfillment. |
| Return the disputed goods | Under return RT-9, the supplier signs for the 2 damaged lot-B bottles and the 3 other-variant bottles. The shop now holds **54**, all saleable. | The owner requests six regular bottles, comprising four undelivered and two replacements. The supplier-credit decision has its own reference. |
| Complete the expectation | A later truck delivers six regular bottles under receipt R-46. All six pass inspection. Available stock reaches **60**. | Record the second delivery and close the outstanding six units. A duplicate copy of D-71 opens R-41 for inspection and produces zero movement. |

**Proof for the next shift.** Hand the next receiver only the order, R-41, RT-9 and R-46. They should be able to trace the two damaged lot-B bottles and the three unapproved substitutes to the signed return. Their reconciliation should show 59 received on the first truck, five returned and six received later, leaving 60. At the earlier shift change before R-46, they should find 54 on hand and six awaiting the supplier. If the shop runs an administrative package such as Saint, confirm which existing receipt or purchase document already posts stock before enabling any connector write.
